# TCGE Coupling Axiom

Date: 2026-09-25
Status: Proposed formal derived construct, pending independent Echo validation

## Canonical TCGE theorem

K = R AND I AND E
H = I AND NOT K

Where:
- R = Reality / grounding
- I = Inference
- E = Echo / independent validation
- K = validated knowledge
- H = inference without validated-knowledge status

## Coupled states

Let:

X = (R_X, I_X, E_X)
Y = (R_Y, I_Y, E_Y)

Define coupling C(X,Y) as an admissible relationship or transfer between X and Y:

C(X,Y) = 1 iff X <-> Y through a defined relation.

## Coupling boundary

Coupling alone does not establish knowledge:

C(X,Y) does not imply K.

The canonical TCGE knowledge gate remains:

K = R AND I AND E.

## Validated coupling

Define:

C_K(X,Y) = C(X,Y) AND R AND I AND E

Therefore:

C_K = C AND K.

### TCGE Coupling Axiom

C AND K = C_K.

Connection is not validation.

## Unvalidated inferential coupling

Define:

C_H = C AND I AND NOT K.

Because:

H = I AND NOT K,

therefore:

C_H = C AND H.

## Non-inferential coupling

Define:

C_0 = C AND NOT I.

## Coupling partition

C ->
  C_K = C AND K       : validated coupling
  C_H = C AND H       : unvalidated inferential coupling
  C_0 = C AND NOT I   : non-inferential coupling

## Interpretation

Coupling transports or relates state. TCGE determines whether an inference crossing or existing within that coupling qualifies as validated knowledge.

The existence of a connection, transfer, repository record, agreement, repetition, or correlation must not independently set E=1.

## Governance status

This coupling axiom is a proposed extension derived from the canonical TCGE Boolean framework. Its repository preservation and internal Boolean consistency do not by themselves constitute independent Echo validation.
