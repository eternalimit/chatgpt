DOCUTECH — Governed Artifact Definition

Canonical role: DOCUTECH is the document-engineering and finalization layer that converts a governed research state into a durable, human-readable, audit-ready artifact without changing the underlying evidence state.

Functional boundary

DOCUTECH may structure, render, index, package, and preserve a result. It does not create Reality evidence, independent Echo, scientific validation, legal notarization, or authorization merely by rendering or hashing an artifact.

Research-chain placement

GIVE₀ → GET → THROUGH → GATE → GIVE₁ → ECHO
                                      ↓
                           NOTARY → LEDGER → DOCUTECH

The placement is a research definition for this artifact. ECHO, NOTARY, LEDGER, and DOCUTECH remain distinct functions.

DOCUTECH artifact contract

  1. Input: a frozen governed state plus its provenance, unresolved conditions, and authorized output scope.
  2. Preserve: source identity, historical boundaries, evidence classifications, and unresolved conditions.
  3. Render: a clear decision-ready document or machine-readable artifact.
  4. Hash: compute a digest over the exact output bytes after rendering.
  5. Verify: independently recompute the digest from the preserved bytes before claiming byte-identity verification.
  6. Return: artifact identity, digest, verification result, provenance, and explicit epistemic limits.

TCGE boundary

A matching hash establishes only the scoped claim that the measured bytes match the referenced digest. It does not by itself establish independent Echo for the artifact's semantic claims. DOCUTECH must not promote a HOLD to PASS or convert an inference into validated knowledge merely through formatting, hashing, or publication.

Notary distinction

Within the reconstructed research architecture, NOTARY witnesses or records the governed event; LEDGER preserves the historical record; DOCUTECH packages the governed result. DOCUTECH is therefore not defined here as the legal or cryptographic notary itself.
