DIFFERENTIAL DYNAMIC FORMULAE

Dynamic GPU Output Artifact

Definition

Differential Dynamic Formulae describe local and instantaneous change inside a governed dynamic system. Differential calculus maps state to its rate of change, while integral calculus reconstructs accumulated state change from that rate.

Formula I — Differential change

dX/dt = lim(Δt→0) [X(t+Δt) − X(t)] / Δt

The derivative is the continuous limiting rate corresponding to finite change ΔX over Δt.

Formula II — State-rate equation

Ẋ(t) = F(X(t), u(t), Γ(t))

The instantaneous state rate is produced by the declared dynamic operator F from state X, input u, and context/governance Γ.

Formula III — Differential state transition

dN/dt = Ṅ(t)
dN = Ṅ(t) dt

This separates an instantaneous rate from an infinitesimal state change.

Formula IV — Differential angular dynamics

dθ/dt = ω(t)
z(t) = exp(iθ(t))
dz/dt = iω(t) exp(iθ(t))

Angular velocity ω is the local rate of phase change. The complex representation differentiates according to the chain rule.

Formula V — Differential gated dynamics

dV/dt = GATE(V(t), X(t), Γ(t))

This is a candidate governed continuous-state representation. GATE must be separately defined; the expression is not asserted as a physical law.

Formula VII — Differential / integral duality

dX/dt = F(X(t),u(t),Γ(t))
X(t) = X(t₀) + ∫[t₀→t] F(X(τ),u(τ),Γ(τ)) dτ

The differential form specifies local evolution. The integral form accumulates that evolution over an interval.

𝒰 is the unified dynamic state, u is input/control, Γ is context/governance, and 𝔉 is the declared dynamic operator.
